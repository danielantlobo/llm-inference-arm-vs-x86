1. Memory footprint: When training transformers on CPU, there is a large memory footprint as the models are made up of millions of parameters, which require significant memory. Quantisation techniques have been proposed to reduce the memory footprint while still allowing for the model to be trained and inferred efficiently. For example, the 'SqueezeNet' and 'MobileNet' architectures in the paper 'Deep SqueezeNet' by Hari et al. In 2017 use quantisation to reduce the memory footprint of their models, while maintaining good inference performance.

Repeated loading of weights during decoding: As mentioned earlier, when deploying transformers on CPU-only systems, the model's large memory footprint makes repeated loading of weights during the decoding phase a challenge. Quantisation techniques can help mitigate this challenge by reducing the size of the model's weights to ensure that they can be loaded quickly during the decoding process.
